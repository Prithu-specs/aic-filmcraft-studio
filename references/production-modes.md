# Production modes

Choose a mode at intake, then upgrade when scope, cost, team size, release risk, or continuity demand increases.

| Requirement | Mini: 30 sec–3 min | Short: 3–15 min | Long: >15 min |
|---|---|---|---|
| Core story record | one-sentence promise + beat map | full brief, character sheets, timed scene map | full brief, sequence map, character/relationship arcs |
| Canon | compact, versioned | complete, versioned asset registry | complete canon plus sequence-specific addenda |
| Planning | shot cards | timed scene packets and coverage plan | scene packets, sequence boards, dependencies |
| Review | every clip and final master | clip, seam, cut, sound, master | same, plus sequence and recovery checkpoints |
| Change control | record material changes | impact list required | impact, schedule, budget, approval and archive update required |
| Roles | separate creative and QA passes | named owner for each gate | named owner/integrator and selected specialist lanes |

## Mini mode

Use for a reel, ad, teaser, proof-of-concept, 30-second story, 1-minute video, or 3-minute short. Required artifacts: compact brief, beat map, mini canon, shot cards, attempt log, clip review, edit plan, master/delivery record. Do not skip source/voice/music rights or caption checks.

## Short mode

Use for a story that needs scenes, performance changes, continuity, or a 3–15 minute runtime. Required artifacts: brief, script, character and visual canon, asset registry, scene packets, preflight ledger, clip/seam reviews, edit decision list, sound spotting plan, picture lock, master QA, delivery and archive record.

## Long mode

Use for a feature, episodic work, or a complex film over 15 minutes. Divide the film into sequences with a beginning, pressure, turn, and handoff. Maintain a dependency map, schedule, budget reserve, selected specialist coverage, canonical asset source, change log, backups, and regular project recovery tests. Retain the same gates as Short mode; increase depth only where the project needs it.

## Escalation triggers

Upgrade a project’s control level when any of these is true:

- a character, recurring prop, or location appears across multiple scenes;
- a paid generator, deadline, or public client release raises financial risk;
- multiple people or AI roles can create conflicting versions;
- the release has music, voice, likeness, copyright, accessibility, or competition rules; or
- a scene cannot be judged from a single generated clip because the edit depends on joins, sound, or continuity.
